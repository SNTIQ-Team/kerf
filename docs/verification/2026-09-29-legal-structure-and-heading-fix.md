# Legal structure and court heading corrections — 2026-09-29

## Legal units (`law-structure/1.1`)

A sentence can begin with `§`, a number, or a quoted/parenthesized statement. Abbreviation, citation, and calendar-ordinal guards still run. Spaced list markers inspect their immediate source separator rather than a whitespace-stripped preceding word. Explicit Bavarian sentence numbering bounds each numbered list separately, allowing Nr. 1 to restart in the next sentence.

Regression fixtures are public, source-bound bodies of § 74 AsylG and Art. 39 BayDSG from the 2026-09-29 audit. The former has sentence counts 4/4/2; Abs. 1 Satz 4 begins with § 58 and Abs. 2 Satz 2 with § 87b. The latter has 16 Nummern, including Abs. 1 Satz 2 Nr. 3. Input law wording, text hashes, historical validity status, and source attribution are unchanged. Only the structural projection is corrected. Structure is generated by `norm_history` on read; restarting the API loads the revised parser rather than editing canonical texts.

## Court units (`courts-parser/1.8.1`)

An explicitly unnumbered heading closes the preceding Rn and receives an unnumbered positional anchor. A heading with its own source Rn retains it. Ordinary unnumbered continuation blocks still belong to the preceding Rn. RII sometimes marks a Roman section label only as `<dd><p>II</p></dd>`; such standalone unnumbered labels are classified as headings.

The original BVerwG 1 B 19.16 fixture still yields Rn. 10 with its three source blocks, Rn. 6 with four, and the complete unchanged sequence Rn. 1–12. Heading II is separate from Rn. 2. Equivalent expectations were corrected in the existing BVerwG and BVerfG fixtures; quote-location assertions remain enabled.

## Completeness

The Python search reader treats failed items and non-ok source status as incomplete even if the pending queue is empty. This matches the MCP reader and avoids a false complete-negative result.
